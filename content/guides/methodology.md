## Principles

1. **A modpack is a designed experience, not a mod list.** We evaluate what a pack *does to the game*, not how many jars it ships.
2. **Judge packs against their own promise.** A cozy pack is not penalised for lacking a tech tree; a kitchen sink is not penalised for lacking a story. Every score asks "how well does this deliver what its audience came for?"
3. **Criticism must be specific and sourced.** Cons sections are compiled from the most-upvoted critical threads, the official issue trackers, and pack descriptions themselves — not from vibes.
4. **Currency matters.** Every review states the version, loader, status and "last revised" date. Modpacks change; a 2023 review of Prominence II is not a 2026 review.
5. **Independence.** No affiliate links, no hosting sponsors, no pack team involvement. The [footer](../index.html) says it and we mean it.

## The seven metrics

Each pack receives 1–10 on seven axes. The overall score is a weighted mean (weights in brackets) rounded to one decimal.

| Metric | Weight | What a 10 looks like | What a 2 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Content depth** | 1.5 | Hundreds of hours of *distinct* things to do; custom content; nothing feels padded | A single mod with a launcher; or 400 mods you never touch |
| **Polish & stability** | 1.2 | Rare crashes, coherent configs, no duplicate ores, active fixes, clean UI | Recipe conflicts, crafting lag, broken quests, abandoned bugs |
| **Progression design** | 1.5 | Gates that teach, pacing that rewards, an endgame that lands | No gating at all, or gating by tedium; endgame walls the early game never prepared you for |
| **Performance** | 0.8 | Runs on 4 GB with shaders; loads in seconds | Needs 12 GB; 5-minute loads; stutters on a modern CPU (higher score = *lighter*) |
| **Beginner friendliness** | 0.6 | A vanilla player can start and have fun without a wiki | Assumes you know AE2 P2P tunnels and GregTech circuit lines |
| **Multiplayer suitability** | 0.8 | Shared goals, team systems, scales to a server, divides labour well | Single-base design, per-player grind, TPS collapse with 4 players |
| **Longevity / replay** | 1.0 | Years of updates, alternate starts/modes, reasons to return | Complete in a weekend; frozen; no variants |

Weights reflect what players tell us matters most when they *finish or abandon* a pack: depth and progression design dominate; beginner-friendliness is lowest because we already route beginners to appropriate packs.

**Performance is inverted on purpose** — a higher number means a lighter pack — so that "higher is better" holds across all columns of the [comparison table](../compare.html).

## Tiers

Tiers are assigned by hand, informed by but not derived from the score, and answer one question: *how strongly do we recommend this pack to the audience it targets?*

- **S** — Best in class. If this is your category, play this. (ATM10 for sinks, GTNH for expert, StoneBlock 4 for questing, COBBLEVERSE for Pokémon…)
- **A** — Excellent; recommended with a specific caveat (version, hardware, audience, unfinished endgame).
- **B** — Good with real reservations; read the cons before committing.
- **C** — Included for completeness or historical importance; better options exist. (None of the 41 currently reviewed packs are C-tier — packs we'd rate C were cut from the list.)

An S-tier cozy pack and an S-tier expert pack are both S because both are the best answer to their question. Comparing tiers across categories is meaningless; comparing scores across categories is *mostly* meaningless. Use them within a category.

## How playtime, RAM and difficulty are estimated

- **Playtime** is the range between "focused player who knows the mods" and "typical player who wanders", drawn from pack wikis, finish-post threads (e.g. "finished DJ2 after 540 hours"), and official estimates (GTNH wiki: 1,600 h team / 2,500 h solo).
- **RAM** is *client allocation for a comfortable experience without shaders*, taken from pack pages where stated and community reports otherwise. Add 1–2 GB for shaders.
- **Difficulty** uses a six-step vocabulary: Beginner · Easy · Moderate · Hard · Expert · Brutal. It measures recipe/progression demand and survival punishment, not mod count. Community difficulty-ladder threads (2023, 2024, 2026) were the primary calibration source.

## Sources

Reviews draw on: official pack pages (CurseForge, Modrinth, FTB, GitHub), pack wikis, the r/feedthebeast archive (with particular weight on high-upvote threads: recommendation megathreads, finish reviews, critical reviews and their replies), pack-specific subreddits, monthly popularity data from hosting sites (for download and ranking figures), and long-form community comparison guides. Where a claim is contested we present both sides. Where a figure is dated (download counts, version numbers) we say when it was captured.

Research notes with source URLs live in the [repository](https://github.com/gorg667/modpacks-research/tree/main/research) for anyone who wants to check our work.

## What this guide deliberately does not do

- **Rank by downloads.** Downloads measure marketing, age and thumbnail quality.
- **Review every pack.** Thousands exist; we cover the ones with consensus quality, historical weight or 2026 momentum, and we'd rather write 40 deep reviews than 400 shallow ones.
- **Pretend to be neutral about design.** We believe gating that teaches beats gating by quantity; that cohesion beats mod count; that a finish line makes a pack better. Those are opinions, argued in each review.
- **Rate mods.** This is about packs. A great mod in a bad pack is still a bad pack.

## Corrections

Facts drift. If a pack has updated in a way that changes a review, or if we got something wrong, [open an issue](https://github.com/gorg667/modpacks-research/issues) with a source. Revisions are noted at the bottom of each review.

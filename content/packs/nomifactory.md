## The short version

Nomifactory is the spiritual successor to Omnifactory, built by former Omnifactory community members, and it has one of the clearest design statements in modded Minecraft: **a quest-driven expert pack about building large-scale factories, with GregTech's complexity and none of its grind.** No steam age (you're fast-tracked into LV, "where things get really interesting"), no magic, no forced combat (Peaceful is fully supported), resource generation unlocked early (Deep Mob Learning, oil rigs, micro miners), AE2 without channels, RF/EU conversion so you can use Thermal and EnderIO for power plumbing — and a quest book that is "a veritable encyclopedia" rather than a checklist.

The result is the pack every difficulty-ladder thread names as the step before GTNH ("e2e > nomi > gtnh"), and the pack the GTNH wiki itself cites as invaluable preparation. It is also, remarkably, playable on **3–4 GB of RAM**.

## Two flavours

- **Nomifactory** (the original): GregTech Community Edition + Shadows of Greg; stable 1.2.x with 1.3 in long development. Compatible with old Omnifactory worlds.
- **Nomi-CEu**: a fork using **GregTech CEu**, actively developed, with **Normal, Hard and Expert modes** (Hard adds the steam age, no Hostile Neural Networks, unspendable coins; Expert forces GT-only power and re-enables explosions). In 2026 Nomi-CEu is the version most people actually play, and the base for Monifactory's modern port.

## What the game is

Punch trees → basic machines → LV → MV → HV → EV → IV → LuV → ZPM → UV → and onward to **creative items in survival**. Each tier: new circuits, new materials, new multiblocks, new bottlenecks. Because resources are effectively infinite early, the entire game is *design*: how do you turn ore into thousands of circuits per hour, keep platinum-group processing fed, route fluids without a spaghetti nightmare, and autocraft it all with AE2? Deep Mob Learning gives mob drops without mob farms; Micro Miners (custom rockets) fetch off-world materials; the Solar and Naquadah lines eventually power a city.

Playtime lands around 300–600 hours — comparable to Divine Journey 2, a fifth of GTNH — and the pack has a definite end (creative tank/chest/etc.), unlike its big sibling.

## Where it falls short

It's 1.12.2 (Cleanroom-era Java 17+ works in Nomi-CEu and helps performance a lot). It has zero adventure or narrative; the world is a resource sheet. And GregTech is still GregTech: circuit assembly lines, polymer chains and platinum processing are long and require real planning, even with the nerfs removed. If "Only Factories!" doesn't sound like a promise, this isn't your pack.

## How it compares

- **vs GTNH:** Nomi is the tutorial. Same grammar (tiers, circuits, multiblocks), a fraction of the length, none of the survival, no rockets-to-space.
- **vs Monifactory (1.20.1):** Moni is Nomi-CEu ported and remastered on GTCEu Modern, with sculk mechanics replacing Draconic/Avaritia and a new UV+ progression. Pick Moni for modern Minecraft, Nomi for the proven original and lower RAM.
- **vs Enigmatica 2: Expert:** E2E is broader (magic + tech), less Greg, and the easier first expert pack; Nomi is the better *second*.
- **vs StaTech Industry:** Fabric, Modern Industrialization instead of GregTech, easier, polished — the lightest step into factory packs.

## Verdict

Nomifactory (and Nomi-CEu) is S-tier because it does exactly what it promises for exactly the audience it targets: it turns GregTech from a punishment into a puzzle, teaches it superbly, runs on anything, and delivers hundreds of hours of the best factory-building in Minecraft. If GTNH is Everest, this is the training climb that makes Everest possible — and a great mountain in its own right.

## The short version

GregTech: New Horizons is not a modpack in the way ATM10 or Better Minecraft are modpacks. It is closer to a **separate game built on Minecraft's engine**, developed continuously for over a decade by a team that has forked, patched and modernised essentially the entire 1.7.10 modding ecosystem to serve one vision: a progression so deep and interconnected that reaching the end is a multi-year, multi-person accomplishment.

Its reputation precedes it. The r/feedthebeast running joke — "it's this new innovative pack that combines tech and magic… I think it's called GregTech: New Horizons" — topped the 2026 "best modpacks" thread with nearly 100 upvotes, and every difficulty-ranking thread for the last eight years ends with the same name. There is GTNH, and then there is everything else.

## What GTNH actually is

The premise: take GregTech 5 Unofficial — a mod that replaces Minecraft's material system with a realistic chemistry and voltage-tiered machine ladder — and **greg-ify everything else**. Vanilla recipes, Thaumcraft, Tinkers' Construct, Forestry bees, Railcraft, Applied Energistics, IndustrialCraft 2, Botania, Blood Magic, Witchery, Galacticraft, EnderIO, Pam's HarvestCraft, and hundreds more: every recipe is rebalanced so nothing can be made before the technology tier where it makes sense. A wooden door needs a saw. A crafting table needs flint. Your first furnace needs bricks you have to find clay for. Steel needs a multiblock Bricked Blast Furnace and hours of charcoal. And that is the first ten hours.

From there the pack climbs **sixteen tiers**: Stone Age → Steam → LV → MV → HV → EV → IV → LuV → ZPM → UV → UHV → UEV → UIV → UMV → UXV → MAX. Each electrical tier delivers four times the voltage of the last and unlocks new multiblocks (Electric Blast Furnace, Vacuum Freezer, Large Chemical Reactor, Distillation Tower, Fusion Reactor Mk I–III, Assembly Line, Research Station, Eye of Harmony…), new materials, new dimensions via Galacticraft rockets (Moon, Mars, asteroids, and far beyond), and new automation puzzles. Magic mods are woven into the same ladder — Thaumcraft's research gives you essentials at MV, Blood Magic and Botania unlock at HV/EV, Witchery has its own late-game utility — so "I'll just play a mage" is not an escape hatch; the wiki's line is that "even a mage needs a technological base."

The endgame is the **Stargate**: a construction so absurd (its components require every tier's output at industrial scale) that as of 2026 only a handful of players and servers have built one. The wiki explicitly says most players will never see it and that this is fine.

## The early game is where dreams die

GTNH's first 20–40 hours are its most controversial. You will:

- Hunt for **ore veins** across a custom "Realistic Alpha" world (Biomes O' Plenty + Realistic World Gen), because GregTech ores spawn in dense localized veins rather than everywhere. Finding your first tin or copper can take an hour.
- Fight a **food system** (Spice of Life, Hunger Overhaul, Pam's) where steak barely feeds you and dietary variety literally increases your max HP.
- Survive **Infernal Mobs**, **Blood Moons**, exploding animals, quicksand, Thaumcraft Hungry Nodes and taint, and mobs that punch through your wooden walls.
- Learn that tools have durability, mining levels and *tiered* mining speeds via Tinkers'.
- Read. A lot. The quest book is dense, accurate, and non-optional.

The pack does give you an excellent ramp — quests hand out early food, a first iron pickaxe head, and lore-friendly hints — and there are dozens of guides. But the honest advice from the community is unambiguous: **if this is your first tech pack, don't.** Play Nomifactory, Monifactory or Enigmatica 2: Expert first to learn how GregTech "thinks" (ore processing chains, byproducts, circuits, batch crafting, multiblock maintenance). The wiki says the same.

## Why it's still S-tier despite all that

**Engineering quality.** No other pack team has done what GTNH's has: they maintain GT5U itself, forked NEI into the best recipe browser on any version, wrote *lwjgl3ify* so 1.7.10 runs on Java 21 with modern LWJGL (fixing a decade of rendering and input bugs), integrated Angelica (a Sodium-like renderer for 1.7.10) with shader support, and patched hundreds of mods with fixes that upstream abandoned years ago. The result is a 1.7.10 pack that runs *better* and more stably than many 1.20 packs.

**Progression design.** Every tier has a bottleneck that forces a new kind of thinking — the first EBF forces power scaling; HV forces oil processing; EV/IV force you to automate circuits properly; LuV introduces the Assembly Line and mass-production mindset; ZPM+ is about logistics at scale. Nothing is arbitrary; everything is documented; and the game keeps teaching you real-adjacent chemistry (you *will* learn what a Fischer-Tropsch process is).

**Living development.** 2.7.0 (December 2024, the 10-year anniversary) was the largest update in the pack's history; 2.8.x stables arrived through 2025; 2.9 betas are running now. The team reworks entire tiers based on player data. This is not a museum piece.

**Community.** The Discord is one of the most active in modded Minecraft, the wiki is a genuine encyclopedia (per-tier guides, chemistry flowcharts, power calculators), and there is a decade of let's-plays — Kharax82's multi-thousand-hour series being the canonical one.

## Time investment, in numbers

The official wiki estimate: **~1,600 hours for a team, ~2,500 hours for an experienced solo player** to reach the endgame chapters. Documented full clears include a solo player logging **3,990 hours** to complete the pack in August 2025. The Stargate is generally cited at **4,000–8,000 hours** depending on team size. For calibration: Divine Journey 2 is ~500 hours, Nomifactory ~300–500, ATM10's Star ~150–300.

Most people play GTNH not to finish but to *reach a tier*. Getting to HV and launching your first rocket (~200–400 hours) is a complete, satisfying arc on its own, and the pack is designed so every tier has its own dopamine.

## Performance & setup

- **Use Java 17–21+ via lwjgl3ify** (the official download is pre-configured for it). Startup is 3–5 minutes on a fast PC; the sandbox-friendly Java 8 path is slower and less stable.
- **Do not use the CurseForge launcher.** The wiki is explicit: the listing is broken and out of date. Use **Prism Launcher** (recommended), MultiMC, ATLauncher or Technic. The official site has one-click zips for each.
- 6–8 GB of RAM is plenty; more is unnecessary and can hurt GC.
- Angelica shaders work; expect a hit on weak GPUs.
- Servers: 8–12 GB, and strongly recommended over singleplayer — ore hunting and infrastructure scale much better with a team.

## How it compares

- **vs Nomifactory / Monifactory:** those are "GregTech without the survival and without the rockets", 300–600 hours, on 1.12.2 / 1.20.1. They are the correct on-ramp.
- **vs TerraFirmaGreg:** TFG fuses TFC's realistic survival with GTCEu on 1.20.1 — arguably more brutal early, less deep late (no defined endgame yet), modern version.
- **vs Meatballcraft:** similar total length, far more exploration and custom content, less grind, 1.12.2.
- **vs Divine Journey 2:** DJ2 is the "hardest non-Greg" pack; think of it as one-fifth of GTNH's runtime with a magic focus.

## Verdict

GTNH is the only pack on this site where "should I play it?" is a lifestyle question rather than a gaming one. If you have finished an expert pack and wished it were five times longer, if you enjoy the *idea* of a factory more than the loot, if you have a group willing to commit — there is nothing else like it and there probably never will be again. Everyone else should admire it from a distance, watch a let's-play, and start with Nomifactory.

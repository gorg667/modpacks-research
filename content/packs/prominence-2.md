## The short version

Prominence II is the RPG pack everyone has seen — it lives permanently in CurseForge's top 10, has 16 M+ downloads across listings, and its trailers look like a AAA fantasy game. It is also the subject of the most upvoted negative modpack review in r/feedthebeast's history ("Playing Prominence II is an abysmal experience", 346 upvotes, 98% ratio, May 2025), and the top comments in that thread are veterans nodding along.

Both things are true. Prominence II is ambitious, beautiful and *fun with friends*, and it is also a kitchen sink wearing an RPG's clothing, with balance and configuration problems the team has spent two years patching. The 4.0 "Kingdom of Vaaz" update (August 2026) is its biggest step yet toward the game it wants to be. We rate it B: strongly recommended for the right group, with eyes open.

## What it is

A **Fabric 1.20.1** pack of roughly 300 mods built around an RPG spine: **levels and a talent tree** (points every two levels; 10 fates/classes, passives, actives, 100+ stat talents), **relic/artifact weapons** you forge and upgrade, **transmogrification**, **multiplayer mob scaling**, and a custom quest/story system that unlocks progressively through two storylines (the original campaign and the *Void's Invasion* expansion). Combat comes from Better Combat + Spell Engine (Paladins & Priests, Wizards, Archers), with weapons from Simply Swords and a large roster of bosses from **Bosses of Mass Destruction**, **MineCells**, **Mowzie's Mobs**, **L_Ender's Cataclysm** and custom encounters. Endgame is the **Mythic Challenges** ladder, reworked in 4.0 into instanced, timed arenas with affixes.

**4.0 Kingdom of Vaaz** adds a handcrafted capital city that physically evolves as you complete quests, voice-acted NPCs, shops and taverns, a proper tutorial, two new classes with unique spellbooks and armour, Primordial endgame armour, and the Mythic rework. The May 2026 patch before it reworked the economy and added two biomes and an endgame storyline. This is a team shipping real content at a real pace.

Around the RPG core sits a large exploration layer (Terralith/BYG-style biomes, YUNG's structures, When Dungeons Arise, Cataclysm structures) and — controversially — a tech layer: **Botania, Create, Applied Energistics 2, Powah, Tech Reborn, Ad Astra**. Modern Industrialization was in early versions and has since been gutted.

## The case against

The 2025 mega-thread and its 100+ comments are worth reading in full, but the arguments cluster:

1. **Theme incoherence.** "Let me build a nuclear reactor in my medieval castle and launch Apollo from my wizard tower." The tech mods are optional, but they litter mines with unfamiliar ores, bloat JEI, and — because mod names under items are hidden for immersion — make it hard to even know what belongs to what.
2. **Configuration debt.** Multiple conflicting smelting recipes; Polymorph not covering Create fan-smelting; deprecated mods and duplicate dependencies in the manifest; and the infamous **crafting-table lag**, which a player traced to two compat mods (Mowzie's × Cataclysm compat among them) — removing them fixed it instantly, and the fix was reportedly deleted from the Discord.
3. **Boss balance.** The chapter-one Nether boss has a one-shot at its recommended level. The Night Lich teleports constantly and is miserable for melee without a flying mount (whose spawn biome, per one player, no longer exists). The Gauntlet can path out of its arena and regenerate. Late bosses (Conceierge, THENULL) get better reviews but remain "stand still and one-shot you" designs for some.
4. **Classes as marketing.** Rogue armour needs 14 rabbit hides for gear that lasts one fight; the class-specific third tier has no visible recipe; most players end up in whatever has the highest armour value with legendary rerolls.
5. **Quests that narrate rather than teach.** Beautiful lore, little guidance; the 4.0 tutorial city is the direct response.

## The case for

The same threads contain the counter-argument, and it is not nothing: groups that play together for months, players who "always knew what to do next", the sensation of a proper Minecraft Dungeons sequel, stats that visibly matter, transmog and relics that let you build an identity, a Botania path that is genuinely satisfying as the pack's "tech". One reviewer's 6/10-with-friends, 4/10-solo summary is about right for versions before 4.0; Vaaz raises the floor.

It also *looks* like nothing else. The main menu, the UI skin, the custom soundtrack, the colour grading — if presentation is a large part of why you play, Prominence delivers more of it than any competitor.

## Performance & setup

6 GB minimum, 8 GB recommended with the bundled shaders; Fabric + Sodium keeps FPS reasonable on mid-range hardware, but crafting/JEI lag on some versions is a real known issue (see above). Servers need 8 GB+ and benefit hugely from the mob-scaling design. Available on both CurseForge and Modrinth.

## How it compares

- **vs Craft to Exile 2:** CtE2 is the *systems* RPG — Mine and Slash, PoE talent web, instanced maps, loot filters, an actual Atlas endgame. Less pretty, far more coherent.
- **vs DawnCraft:** DawnCraft is jankier, souls-like, on 1.18.2, with a more infamous performance profile; Prominence is the more polished of the two.
- **vs Integrated MC:** IMC is the pick if the Prominence thread's criticisms resonate — cohesive, grand structures, well-integrated mods, less "campaign".
- **vs Cisco's Fantasy Medieval RPG:** no tech at all, Skyrim-flavoured, but unbalanced one-shots of its own.

## Verdict

Prominence II: Hasturian Era is the most ambitious RPG modpack on modern Minecraft and, after 4.0, a substantially better one than the pack that drew the 2025 pile-on. It remains a pack whose flaws — tech-mod incoherence, config debt, boss swinginess — are structural rather than cosmetic. Play it with three friends on a decent server, ignore the tech mods, lean on the Discord and the wiki, and you'll have a memorable few months. Play it solo expecting the trailer, and you may end up writing the next Reddit post.

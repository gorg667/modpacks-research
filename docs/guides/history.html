A history of modpacks & the hall of fame

From Tekkit to StoneBlock 4: the eras of modded Minecraft, the packs that defined them, and short write-ups of legends that no longer get full reviews.

Why history matters when choosing a pack

Every pack on this site descends from something. SevTech's ages descend from Blightfall's staging; StoneBlock 4's Unearthers are a reaction to a decade of Ex Nihilo sieves; ATM10's Star descends from Infinity Evolved's Expert Mode; every RPG pack is trying to be the Hexxit its designers remember. Knowing the lineage helps you understand what a pack is trying to do — and lets you go back and play the originals, most of which still run.

The eras

2011–2013: the beginning — Tekkit, Technic, FTB

Modpacks began as manually assembled folders. Technic and Tekkit (IndustrialCraft, BuildCraft, RedPower, Equivalent Exchange) made "modded Minecraft" a phrase; the Technic Launcher (2012) made it installable. Feed The Beast started as a skyblock challenge map by Slowpoke that required a specific mod set; the FTB Launcher (late 2012) and packs like FTB Ultimate and Direwolf20's pack followed. Mod permissions drama between Technic and mod authors shaped the community's norms for a decade. Hexxit (2013) — Technic's adventure pack of dungeons, Twilight Forest and Better Dungeons — became the template for every RPG pack since and, for many players, the most nostalgic pack ever made. Yogscast Complete (2014) was the first mega-kitchen-sink and the ancestor of ATM.

2013–2015: the map packs and the birth of design — 1.6.4 / 1.7.10

Agrarian Skies (Jadedcat, 2014) was the first designed skyblock: a Hardcore Questing Mode quest book, custom recipes, a goal. It invented the genre StoneBlock 4 belongs to. Crash Landing (Iskandar, 2014) — you crash on a desert planet with thirst and heat, and must bootstrap tech to escape — was the first survival-progression pack and the ancestor of OceanBlock 2's premise. Blightfall (Talonos, 2015) put you on a Thaumcraft-tainted planet with a story, a ship, and stage-gated progression; it is still cited as the best narrative pack ever shipped and is a direct ancestor of SevTech. Regrowth (thephoenixlodge, 2015) — restore a dead world through magic and agriculture — was the model for Enigmatica 9: Expert's premise. Material Energy^4, Blood N Bones (the RLCraft of its day, on 1.6.4) and Infinity Evolved (FTB, 2015) — whose Expert Mode was the first mainstream "expert pack" — complete the era. And in 2015, on 1.7.10, a German team began greg-ifying everything: GregTech: New Horizons 1.0.

2016–2019: the 1.12.2 golden age

1.12.2 stayed the modding target long enough for the deepest packs ever made to finish. SevTech: Ages (2018) perfected staged progression. Enigmatica 2: Expert (2018) became the definitive first expert pack. Project Ozone 3 (2018) popularised pack modes. Divine Journey 2 (2021, but a 1.12 project) pushed automation gauntlets to their limit. Omnifactory (2018) then Nomifactory (2020) proved GregTech could be fun. SkyFactory 4 (2019) reinvented skyblock resources. StoneBlock and StoneBlock 2 (2018–19) invented the "encased in stone" variant. RLCraft (2019) became the most downloaded pack ever. Rebirth of the Night started its slow climb. MC Eternal, Roguelike Adventures and Dungeons, Dungeons Dragons and Space Shuttles and GreedyCraft filled out the sink and RPG shelves. GTNH kept growing.

2020–2022: fragmentation — 1.16.5 and 1.18.2

Mojang's update cadence sped up and the ecosystem split. Create (2019–) arrived and, by 1.16, redefined what "tech" meant; Create: Above and Beyond (2021) became the benchmark automation campaign. Enigmatica 6 / 6: Expert (1.16.5) carried the expert torch. Better Minecraft (2020–) began the Vanilla+ boom. Vault Hunters (2020; 3rd Edition 2022) turned a modpack into a roguelike game. Create: Astral (2022) taught a generation Create on Fabric. All the Mods 6/7/8 cemented ATM as the default sink. Fabulously Optimized made Fabric the performance choice. The community began asking why there were no new large expert packs; the honest answer — versions turned over too fast — became a permanent theme.

2023–2024: the 1.20.1 consolidation

1.20.1 became the most complete Forge ecosystem ever. DawnCraft (2023) and Prominence II (2023) made RPG packs the most-downloaded new category. Craft to Exile 2 (2023) offered the systems-first alternative. DeceasedCraft and The Cursed Walking created the modern zombie genre. Integrated MC, Cisco's, and the LPS stable (Medieval MC, Cottage Witch) filled the adventure shelf. All the Mods 9 was the default server pack. Enigmatica 9: Expert (1.19.2) and StaTech Industry (Fabric) were the rare new curated tech packs. Cobblemon (2022–) overtook Pixelmon. MeatballCraft's 2024 announcement reminded everyone that 1.12.2 was still producing masterpieces. Monifactory and TerraFirmaGreg-Modern brought GregTech to 1.20.1.

2025–2026: NeoForge, curation and the return of design

All the Mods 10 on NeoForge 1.21.1 became the biggest pack in the world (21 M+). Create 6 landed and All of Create collected its ecosystem. FTB OceanBlock 2 (Feb 2025) was criticised for weak gating; FTB StoneBlock 4 (Oct 2025) answered with the World Engine, Vaults and Unearthers and became the pack of 2026. Liminal Industries (2025) proved a premise could be a pack. Homestead and Sunlit Valley made cozy a top-10 category. COBBLEVERSE turned Cobblemon into a full RPG. Prominence II 4.0 (Aug 2026) and Craft to Exile 2's 2.0 Atlas (Aug 2026) showed RPG packs maturing. GTNH 2.7 (Dec 2024) celebrated ten years with its biggest update. The 2026 trend, visible in every ranking: curation over mod count, and survival horror as a permanent genre.

Hall of fame: legends without full reviews

These packs shaped the medium. Most still run (Prism Launcher handles old versions well) and several remain excellent; they don't get full reviews because they're retired, superseded, or of mainly historical interest.

Tekkit Classic (1.2.5) / Tekkit 2 (1.12.2) — The original. Tekkit 2 is a faithful modern remake by Technic and a fine nostalgia trip.

Hexxit (1.5.2) / Hexxit Updated (1.12.2) — The adventure pack. Hexxit Updated is a community continuation and the purest "dungeons and Twilight Forest" experience still playable.

Agrarian Skies (1.6.4) — The first designed skyblock. Play it to see where quest books came from.

Crash Landing (1.6.4) — Survive a desert crash with thirst and heat and bootstrap your way off-world. Brutal, brilliant, short. OceanBlock 2 owes it everything.

Blightfall (1.7.10) — The best story pack ever made: a tainted planet, a ship in orbit, stage-gated tech and magic, and a genuine ending. If you can tolerate 1.7.10, it is still a top-tier experience.

Regrowth (1.7.10) — Rebuild a dead world through Botania, Witchery and agriculture. Gentle, magical, unique.

FTB Infinity Evolved (1.7.10) — The kitchen sink with the first great Expert Mode. Still played on servers.

Material Energy^4 / ^5 (1.7.10) — Parcool-style puzzle-map packs; MEΛ5 remains a cult favourite.

Blood N Bones (1.6.4) — The original brutal survival pack, before RLCraft.

Omnifactory (1.12.2) — Nomifactory's direct parent; Nomi is compatible with its worlds and is the version to play.

FTB Interactions (1.12.2) — FTB's GregTech expert pack; excellent, hard, and largely eclipsed by Nomi and GTNH.

GreedyCraft (1.12.2) — Huge Thaumcraft-heavy expert pack with a devoted following.

Dungeons, Dragons and Space Shuttles (1.12.2) — Infamously "hard for hard's sake"; historically important, not recommended.

Enigmatica 6: Expert (1.16.5) — The hardest Enigmatica; only omitted from full review because E2E and E9E bracket it. If you loved E2E and have a year, play it.

StoneBlock 1 & 2 (1.12.2) — The claustrophobic originals SB4 fans miss. Still great.

SkyFactory 2.5 / 3 (1.7.10 / 1.10.2) — The sieve-era skyblocks; SF3's achievement book was ahead of its time.

Roguelike Adventures and Dungeons / RAD 2 (1.12.2 / 1.18.2) — Dungeon-crawl sinks with Epic Fight optional; enjoyable, exhausting long-term.

FTB Academy / University (1.12.2) — Packs designed to teach modded Minecraft. Still the best tutorial packs ever made, if dated.

Rustic Waters II (1.12.2) — An ocean-world progression pack with lore and unusual mechanics; the best non-expert pack many players have never heard of.

Cuboid Outpost (1.16.5) — Compact-machines-in-space questing; a hidden gem.

Decursio Project (1.16.5) / Plastek (1.19.2) / Chroma Endless (1.16.5) — The SevTech descendants: modern staged-progression packs worth a look if ages are your thing.

Supersymmetry (1.12.2) — An in-development GregTech pack aiming to be "the next GTNH"; watch it.

Cosmic Frontiers / Multiblock Madness 2 / Feed the Factory / Stacia 2 Expert — 2024–26 expert-adjacent projects in various states of completion; check their pages before committing.

Lessons the history teaches

  1. Design outlives version. Blightfall, Agrarian Skies and GTNH are on versions a decade old and are still better designed than most 2026 releases.
  2. Every genre has a founding pack, and the founders are usually still worth playing.
  3. Curation is cyclical. Kitchen sinks boom, players tire, designed packs return (2014, 2018, 2025), repeat.
  4. The community's judgment is slow but accurate. Packs that top download charts on launch (DawnCraft, Prominence) get reassessed within a year; packs that grow slowly on word of mouth (Liminal Industries, Rebirth of the Night, MeatballCraft) tend to be the ones people still recommend five years later.

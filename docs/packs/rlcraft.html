What's great

  • Nothing else makes the first night matter like RLCraft: thirst, temperature, hostile everything, and gear gated behind skill levels
  • Ice and Fire dragons, Lycanites' Mobs and Battle Towers create emergent stories players still tell years later
  • Deep hidden systems — Level Up skills, First Aid body parts, Baubles, Reskillable, Locks, Quality Tools — reward mastery
  • Light: runs on 4–6 GB and old hardware
  • Enormous knowledge base — 29 M downloads means every question has a YouTube answer

What holds it back

  • Frozen on 1.12.2 / Java 8 forever; no modern features, old combat, old worldgen
  • Difficulty is often unfair rather than deep: instant deaths from Lycanites mobs, dragons you can't fight for 20 hours, RNG loot
  • Progression is gate-by-frustration (skill levels to hold a sword) rather than designed
  • Once you have dragon armour and good baubles, difficulty collapses and there's little left to do
  • Community is split between veterans and people who quit in the first hour

Play this if…

  • Players who want to be humbled and have stories to tell
  • Content creators — the chaos is a highlight reel machine
  • Small groups who enjoy shared suffering and slow, hard-won progress

Skip it if…

  • You hate losing hours of progress to a random mob
  • You want modern Minecraft or any form of guided progression
  • You get frustrated by systems you have to look up to understand (this pack explains nothing)

The short version

RLCraft is the most downloaded Minecraft modpack in history (29 M+ on CurseForge as of 2026) and one of the very few packs to become a cultural object outside modding — it's the pack YouTubers die in, the pack "ruined" Minecraft for a generation, the pack whose spawn-adjacent dragon is a meme. Created by Shivaxi and finalised at v2.9.3 in 2023, it turns 1.12.2 Minecraft into a punishing survival RPG: thirst and temperature bars, body-part injuries, gear you cannot use until your skills level up, mobs that one-shot you from off-screen, dragons that burn your base, and towers full of loot you're not ready for.

It is not balanced, not modern and not designed in the sense that StoneBlock 4 or Create: Astral are designed. It is a vibe, executed with total commitment, and that's why it endures.

The systems

  • Survival: Simple Difficulty (thirst, temperature — you will die of heatstroke in a desert and freeze in snow without appropriate armour/drinks), First Aid (damage per body part; a broken leg slows you, a head hit at low HP kills you), Lost Cities-adjacent dangers, sleeping requires safety.
  • Progression gates: Level Up! Reloaded and Reskillable — you need points in Attack to wield better swords, in Defence to wear better armour, in Mining to use better picks. Points come from XP levels, so dying (and losing XP) is doubly punishing.
  • Mobs: Lycanites' Mobs (hundreds of creatures from harmless to instant-death; many spawn from events like Blood Moons), Ice and Fire (dragons, sea serpents, hydras, cyclopses, Gorgons that petrify you), Infernal Mobs, Doomlike Dungeons, Battle Towers, Rogue-like Dungeons, Waystones for eventual travel.
  • Gear and RNG: Quality Tools (random item qualities), Baubles (rings, belts, charms with huge effects), Spartan Weaponry (dozens of weapon types with reach and speed), Switch-Bow, dragon-bone weapons and dragon-scale armour as the effective endgame, plus the notorious Sea Serpent scale and Lycanite equipment forge grind.
  • World: Biomes O' Plenty, Dynamic Trees, Recurrent Complex structures, Lost Souls in villages, locks and keys.

There is no quest book. The pack explains nothing; the wiki and 10,000 YouTube videos are the manual.

The experience, honestly

Hours 0–5 are the famous part: punching a tree, realising you can't craft a sword you're allowed to hold, being killed by a rock (a Geonach), being killed by a fire elemental, being killed by a dragon you never saw, starving because you didn't know to make a canteen. Hours 5–30 are the good part: you learn the rules, you find a Battle Tower, you get baubles and a real weapon, you learn which mobs to run from, and every survival feels earned. Hours 30–100 you become a dragon-armoured demigod and the pack goes quiet; there's a Nether/End, there are Lycanites bosses, but most players stop when the fear does.

Whether that's a good arc depends entirely on you. Critics — and there are many — call it "difficulty as randomness": too many deaths are unavoidable, too much progression is skill-point tedium rather than clever design, and the unfair early hours filter out most players. Fans say that's the point, and that no other pack has made a first shelter, a first spear, a first canteen feel like survival.

Versions and variants

  • v2.9.3 (2023) — final; Trials, Ice and Fire updates, the "Nunchaku" era of balance tweaks. Java 8.
  • RLCraft Dregora — official variant with the enormous Dregora custom-worldgen map; recommended for a second run.
  • Countless community "RLCraft but…" packs (RLCraft Plus, RL2, "Better RLCraft") of varying quality; none are official.
  • Shivaxi has discussed a spiritual successor on a modern version for years; nothing has shipped.

Performance

Old and light: 4 GB works, 6 GB is comfortable. Java 8 only (Java 17+ needs unofficial workarounds). Servers run fine on 6–8 GB; Lycanites events can spike TPS.

How it compares

  • vs Rebirth of the Night: also 1.12.2, also hard — but designed: night gets worse the longer you survive, progression unlocks are deliberate, the atmosphere is dark-fantasy rather than chaos. The thinking person's RLCraft.
  • vs DawnCraft: souls-like combat, 1.18.2, more structure, more jank.
  • vs The Cursed Walking / DeceasedCraft: modern zombie survival-horror; tension from scarcity and hordes rather than dragons.
  • vs TerraFirmaGreg: "hard survival" via realism and tech rather than monsters.

Verdict

RLCraft is A-tier not because it's well-designed — it isn't, particularly — but because it does one thing better than any pack before or since: it makes Minecraft frightening, and it makes surviving feel like an achievement. Everyone should play it once. Most people should stop when the dragon armour goes on. And if you want the same feeling with actual design behind it, Rebirth of the Night is waiting.

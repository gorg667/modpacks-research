What's great

  • The quest book is a genuine curriculum: every step explains what to build, why, and what it unlocks — you finish understanding Create
  • Clear arc from hand-cranked sawmill → steam → brass → trains → rockets → custom Moon, Mars and Mercury (Ad Astra)
  • Custom recipes stay reasonable; challenge comes from designing systems, not from absurd counts
  • Overhauled worldgen and a coherent aesthetic; Fabric performance means 4–6 GB is plenty
  • 1.4 M+ downloads and still-active servers keep the community alive years after release

What holds it back

  • Frozen on Fabric 1.18.2 — no 1.20/1.21 port, so Create 6 features (trains 2.0, new packaging) are absent
  • Late game gets repetitive: rocket-tier materials are ‘scale up the same lines’ rather than new mechanics
  • Some progression hard-gates (e.g. brass) feel slow solo
  • Limited non-Create content; if you don't enjoy Create you will not enjoy this

Play this if…

  • Anyone who wants to *learn* Create properly instead of copying YouTube contraptions
  • Players who found Above and Beyond too punishing or too old
  • Small groups who want a shared factory with a defined end goal
  • Fabric users who want a full progression pack

Skip it if…

  • You only play the newest Minecraft version
  • You want kitchen-sink breadth or magic mods
  • You already know Create inside-out and want a harder challenge (go to Above and Beyond or Arcane Engineering)

The short version

Create: Astral is the pack most Create veterans point new players toward, and after three years it is still the consensus "best Create entry point" — a mid-2026 Create-pack comparison guide put it bluntly: "If you want to learn Create with proper guidance, Create Astral is still the best entry point in 2026 thanks to its outstanding quest book." It is a Fabric 1.18.2 pack of around 150 mods that leads you from a wooden sawmill through steam engines, brass machinery, a train network and finally to rockets and hand-tuned versions of the Moon, Mars and Mercury. It is not the hardest Create pack (that's Above and Beyond), nor the most varied (Arcane Engineering), but it is the best designed for the purpose of teaching.

What makes the quest book special

Most quest books are checklists. Astral's is a textbook with a plot. Each chapter opens with why the next tier matters, each quest tells you what machine to build and what design problem it solves, and the tree is laid out so you can always see where you are and where you're heading. The progression is roughly:

  1. Andesite age — hand cranks, water wheels, mechanical presses, mixers, the first sawmill; you learn stress units and rotational speed by necessity.
  2. Steam & iron — boilers, engines, your first proper factory floor and belt logistics.
  3. Brass — the "real" Create tier: mechanical crafters, deployers, funnels with filters, contraptions; the quest book forces you to automate several multi-step products.
  4. Trains — Create's train system is a full chapter; you build a working rail network to move ore between outposts.
  5. Rockets & space — Ad Astra integration with custom-generated Moon, Mars and Mercury, each with its own resources and hazards, feeding back into more advanced Create automation.

Custom recipes are present but restrained — enough to make you build a production chain, not enough to make you spreadsheet. Sub-80-hour completions are realistic for experienced players; 100–150 hours is typical for first-timers who explore.

The 1.18.2 question

Astral's developers chose to finish the pack on Fabric 1.18.2 (v2.1 shipped in 2025) rather than chase versions. The upside: it is stable and complete, with years of bugfixing. The downside: no Create 6 features, no 1.20 cherry groves or trails-and-tales content, no 1.21 combat/copper changes. If you strongly prefer modern Minecraft, All of Create (NeoForge 1.21.1, Create 6 + 50 addons) is the modern-version alternative — though its quest design is not on Astral's level.

Performance and multiplayer

Fabric plus a lean mod list means Astral runs on almost anything: 4 GB is fine, 6 GB comfortable. Servers for 4–6 players do well on 6–8 GB; the usual Create caveat applies — very large contraptions and dense belt networks cost TPS, which is a server problem, not a client one. Astral is good in small groups because Create factories split naturally into departments (power, ore processing, logistics, space).

How it compares

  • vs Create: Above and Beyond — A&B (Forge 1.16.5) is the genre benchmark and noticeably harder: ~50 inventions to mass-produce, spreadsheets encouraged, 100–200 h. Astral is the friendlier, better-explained sibling.
  • vs Create: Arcane Engineering — CAE (Forge 1.18.2) adds magic (Ars Nouveau, Forbidden & Arcanus) and harder tech (PneumaticCraft, Thermal) in two parallel quest lines. Choose it after Astral or A&B.
  • vs All of Create — modern version, more add-ons, less curriculum.
  • vs Liminal Industries — Create-centric but in a Backrooms survival theme; very different mood.

Verdict

Create: Astral is the standard by which Create packs are measured for teaching quality, and it still holds up in 2026. Its S-tier is earned on progression design — the best of any tech pack of its size — and on polish. The 1.18.2 lock and a slightly flat late game are real, but they don't change the recommendation: if you want to understand Create, play this.

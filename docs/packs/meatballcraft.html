What's great

  • Custom everything: multiblocks, bosses, NPCs, ~70 dimensions (many fully custom), puzzles with recipes hidden in worldgen, hundreds of quests plus a wiki-style in-game reference
  • Elegant expert design: thresholds gate mods but leave their internal recipes intact; early inefficient methods give way to efficient ones instead of being forced forever; staged gating lets you dip into a mod and return
  • Balanced around early infinite resource generation (ProjectE, Mystical Agriculture, Chickens) so the challenge is complex automation, not mining
  • World difficulty scales per chapter — mobs get stronger as you do
  • Beloved by finishers: ‘the ultimate pack’, ‘best Minecraft experience I've ever had’, ‘forced me to learn AE2 autocrafting and P2P’
  • Responsive dev, active Discord, Cleanroom for modern Java performance

What holds it back

  • 1.12.2 — the single most-cited reason people won't try it; no shaders (custom performance setup is Optifine-incompatible)
  • Very RAM-hungry (10 GB+) and needs the recommended JVM arguments or it freezes at memory cleans
  • Early game (~first 20 h) is rough: one-shotting dinosaurs, horror clowns, ghosts; the goofy tone isn't for everyone
  • Heavy on exploration relative to automation for some tastes; structure clutter (improved by a custom Pillar fork)
  • Still ‘beta’: endgame keeps extending; 95% of starters don't reach chapter 4

Play this if…

  • Expert-pack veterans (E2E, DJ2, Nomi) who want *more* without going Greg
  • Players who want tech and adventure in one long-form pack
  • Small teams committing to a single shared base for a year

Skip it if…

  • You won't play 1.12.2 or need shaders
  • You have 8 GB of RAM
  • You want a defined ending soon — the pack is designed to keep growing

The short version

MeatballCraft is the pack veterans mention when someone says "there are no new expert packs". A 749-upvote 2024 announcement thread ("one of the most ambitious modpack projects in a long time") and a steady stream of "obligatory Meatballcraft mention" replies in every 2025–2026 recommendation thread have made it the community's favourite non-GregTech expert pack — with the perpetual caveat that it's on 1.12.2.

It is a tech-RPG: roughly 60% tech, 40% adventure, with early-game magic (Thaumcraft, Astral Sorcery, Botania, Blood Magic). Progression runs through nine chapters (a tenth in development), each with its own lore, quest line and a "chapter book" that unlocks recipes and makes the world harder. Reaching chapter 9's endgame means bases with thousands of machines and automation chains hundreds of steps long. The current content has been finished by only a few dozen people; nearly all took over 1,000 hours.

What makes the design good

The most-upvoted comment in the announcement thread nails three things MeatballCraft does that most expert packs don't:

  1. Messy early, elegant later. You are given inefficient, clunky ways to make things at first — fulfilling the "expert grind" — and then handed better methods in later chapters, instead of being forced to scale the clunky method forever.
  2. Thresholds, not universal pain. Getting into a mod (say, Thermal machine frames) is hard; once you're in, the mod's own recipes are largely untouched. The gate is the challenge, not every craft.
  3. Staged gating. A chapter might require Botania up to pure daisies — not terrasteel. You dip a toe, move on, and come back deeper later.

Add to that: infinite resource generation (ProjectE, Mystical Agriculture, Chickens) is unlocked within hours on purpose, so the pack's difficulty is never "mine 200 million diamonds" but "design the system that turns your infinite basics into this insane item". Custom multiblocks (many repurposing decorative blocks from mods like Twilight Forest), custom bosses (the Meatball Man and his undead horde recur as chapter antagonists), hidden recipes whose hints are scattered in worldgen, and ~70 dimensions to explore round it out. A void world for your base is available early in chapter 2 for performance and megabuilding.

The experience

The first ~20 hours are deliberately hostile (the dev's words) — mobs that one-shot you underground, horror-clown and ghost mobs chasing you home, structure-dense terrain. Some players bounce off this as tonally jarring; the pack does have a goofy streak (it's called MeatballCraft). Past that hump, players describe a rhythm of alternating brain-heavy automation nights with exploration nights — there's always a dimension or puzzle to do when you don't feel like designing a 40-step chain.

The RAM situation is real: 8 GB is not enough; 10–12 GB with the CurseForge-page JVM arguments is the baseline, and the built-in memory cleaner freezes the game if you skimp. Shaders are not possible with the custom performance stack. Cleanroom (a modernised 1.12.2 loader) allows Java 21/22, and Prism Launcher is the recommended host.

How it compares

  • vs GTNH: "different but oddly similar" — constant tiering to faster multiblocks, similar total length, but no GregTech, far less grind, far more exploration and magic, and a goofier tone. The r/ftb difficulty consensus places it one rung below GTNH.
  • vs Divine Journey 2: DJ2 is polished and complete; MBC is bigger, still growing and less grindy (infinite resources early), with a comparable challenge in automation.
  • vs Enigmatica 2: Expert: E2E is the on-ramp; MBC is what you play after.
  • vs Nomifactory / Monifactory: those are Greg-flavoured factory packs with no adventure; MBC is the adventure-flavoured expert pack.

Verdict

MeatballCraft is S-tier for the audience that can run it and will accept 1.12.2: the deepest, most lovingly custom expert pack that isn't GregTech, with design principles that newer packs should study. Its version, its RAM appetite and its brutal opening keep it niche; nothing about its design does.

What's great

  • Unmatched breadth: virtually every major NeoForge tech and magic mod of the 1.21 era in one place
  • The ATM Star / ATM Star “Greg-Star” endgame gives a kitchen sink an actual finish line
  • Extremely stable for its size; fast hotfix cadence and a huge community means every problem is already answered
  • Ideal for servers: everyone can pursue a different mod and still contribute
  • Runs on modern Minecraft (1.21.1) with all the QoL that implies

What holds it back

  • Almost no recipe gating — you can rush Mekanism or Ars Nouveau and trivialise everything else
  • Quest book is a catalogue, not a curriculum; it rarely teaches a mod
  • Heavy: 10–12 GB client RAM realistically, long load times, big servers need 12–16 GB
  • Mod count creates decision paralysis for first-timers and lots of redundant ore/material duplication
  • Endgame is mostly a resource-count grind rather than an engineering challenge

Play this if…

  • Friend groups with mixed interests who want everyone to find “their” mod
  • Players who already know a few mods and want to try the rest
  • Long-running servers that need months of content without custom balancing
  • Anyone who wants the latest versions of Mekanism, Create, AE2, Ars Nouveau, Occultism, Botania…

Skip it if…

  • You want to be challenged by recipes or forced to automate — play an expert pack instead
  • Your PC has 8 GB RAM or less
  • You're brand new to modding and get overwhelmed by choice (start with Better Minecraft or Create: Astral)
  • You want a cohesive theme or story

The short version

All the Mods 10 (ATM10) is the tenth mainline entry in the most downloaded kitchen-sink series in modern Minecraft. As of late summer 2026 it has passed 21 million downloads on CurseForge alone, has held the #1 spot on most monthly popularity charts for the better part of a year, and is the pack that server hosts, YouTubers and Discord servers reach for by default. It runs on Minecraft 1.21.1 with NeoForge (Java 21), bundles roughly 500 mods, and — unusually for a kitchen sink — ships with a proper endgame: the ATM Star, a creative-tier item whose recipe touches nearly every major mod in the pack.

If you want one sentence: ATM10 is the pack you can recommend to almost anyone without being wrong, but it is rarely anyone's favourite pack.

What's actually in it

The ATM philosophy has been the same since ATM1: take everything that is stable and popular, add a healthy dose of newer or lesser-known mods, tweak just enough that nothing crashes or produces 40 kinds of copper, and let players do what they want. In ATM10 that means, in rough clusters:

Tech & automation. Mekanism (the pack's de-facto power and processing backbone), Create 6 with a suite of add-ons, Applied Energistics 2 (plus MEGA cells), Refined Storage, Industrial Foregoing, Thermal series, Powah, Pipez, Modular Routers, Laser IO, Functional Storage, Sophisticated Backpacks/Storage, Extreme Reactors, Big Reactors' spiritual successors, Just Dire Things, Oritech, Integrated Dynamics, ComputerCraft: Tweaked, and the ATM team's own AllTheCompressed / AllTheTweaks / AllTheOres glue mods. The 1.21 era also brought newer names like Actually Additions (returned), Justdirethings, and Replication.

Magic. Ars Nouveau, Botania, Occultism, Iron's Spells 'n Spellbooks, Eidolon: Repraised, Mahou Tsukai, Evilcraft, Forbidden & Arcanus, Malum, Hexerei, Blood Magic (late to arrive, present in newer builds), and the Apotheosis affix/enchanting system that most players treat as the real "gear progression".

Exploration & world. Biomes O' Plenty, Terralith-style worldgen tweaks, Twilight Forest, The Undergarden, Deeper and Darker, The Aether (in some builds), Alex's Mobs, Alex's Caves, YUNG's structures, Mowzie's Mobs, L_Ender's Cataclysm, When Dungeons Arise, Ad Astra (space), and the ATM staple "Mining Dimension" via Allthemodium.

The ATM-exclusive spine. The Allthemodium → Vibranium → Unobtainium ore tier (found in the Other, Nether and End respectively) is the pack's own progression ladder: each metal unlocks armour, tools and — critically — the ingredients for the ATM Star. Combined with Mystical Agriculture (grow anything as a crop), Productive Bees and Chicken/Cows resource gen, this gives ATM its familiar mid-game rhythm: get one unit of a rare material, then automate infinite copies.

Progression, honestly assessed

There is a quest book (FTB Quests), and it is genuinely useful as a map — chapters per mod, reward bags, a clear "here is what ATM Star needs" tree. What it is not is a teacher. A typical ATM quest says "craft a Mekanism Digital Miner" and gives you a loot bag; it does not explain what a miner is for or how to power it. Veterans love this (no hand-holding, no artificial gates); newcomers frequently bounce off the wall of 500 unfamiliar item icons in JEI.

The community's standing joke — repeated in every "why are there no expert packs" thread — is that ATM difficulty is "untweaked Mekanism": within 10–15 hours a competent player has a fusion reactor, digital miners and an ME system, and from there the only remaining wall is the ATM Star's sheer material count. The ATM team knows this and leans into it: ATM is explicitly a sandbox that happens to have a checklist, not a designed progression. Judge it on those terms and it excels; judge it as a "game" and it is shallow.

Time to finish. A focused solo player can craft the ATM Star in ~150 hours; most people who finish take 250–400 hours because they wander. Many never finish and don't care — the pack is a toybox.

The ATM Star, and why it matters

The Star is the reason ATM is a kitchen sink with a point. Its recipe pulls one "trophy" item from each major mod line — Mekanism's antimatter, AE2 singularities, Botania's Gaia spirits, Ars' source gems, Occultism's ritual results, Twilight Forest boss drops, space-tier Ad Astra materials, Allthemodium-family blocks, Productive Bee combs — so completing it requires you to at least touch everything. It transforms "I dabbled in 30 mods" into "I finished a 300-hour project." Newer ATM10 builds added the ATM Star (Greg) variant and a small "creative challenge" tail for the truly committed.

Stability and performance

ATM packs are among the most stable large packs because their team ships weekly-ish updates and their player base finds every crash within hours. The tradeoff is weight: expect 90+ second load times on a fast NVMe machine, 10–12 GB of client RAM allocated for a comfortable experience with shaders off, and a server that wants 12–16 GB for four or more players once bases grow. Chunk generation with the full worldgen suite is noticeably heavier than 1.20.1 packs of similar size. Bundled performance mods (Embeddium/Xenon-class renderers, FerriteCore, ModernFix, Clumps) help, but this is not a laptop pack.

Java 21 is mandatory. Most ATM10 "won't launch" reports are Java 17 installs.

Community, updates and longevity

ATM10 is on its second year of updates and has followed the series pattern: months of "adding mods as they port" followed by a "stability and endgame" phase. The Discord is enormous, the wiki is community-maintained, and every mod in the pack has dedicated tutorial series on YouTube, which is a real advantage for learners. The ATM team also runs spin-offs on the same framework — ATM10: To the Sky (skyblock) and ATMons (Cobblemon) — and ATM 11 chatter will begin whenever the next stable modding version settles.

How it compares

  • vs All the Mods 9 (1.20.1 Forge): ATM9 is lighter, has more mods that never made the 1.21 jump (Thermal/Mekanism in mature states, Blood Magic, more exploration mods), and is the better pick if your PC is average. ATM10 has Create 6, Oritech, better performance mods and the future.
  • vs Direwolf20 1.21 pack: Direwolf's pack is smaller, curated by one person, has no quest book, and updates faster. Choose it if you watch his series or want a lighter "everything" pack.
  • vs FTB StoneBlock 4 / OceanBlock 2: those are designed progression packs with custom mechanics; ATM is a sandbox. Different products.
  • vs Enigmatica 10: E10 uses a similar mod list with more integration work and unified resources; it is the "curated ATM". Still catching up on content depth.
  • vs Prominence II / RPG packs: ATM has better tech, worse combat and no story.

Recommended setup

  • Allocate 10 GB (12 on 32 GB machines). Do not allocate more than ~50% of system RAM.
  • Use the CurseForge app, Prism Launcher or the FTB App. Modrinth App also works with the Modrinth listing.
  • Disable Alex's Caves cave biomes or Terralith density if chunk gen stutters on an older CPU.
  • For servers: 12–16 GB, use Aikar's flags, and pre-generate ~2,500 blocks with Chunky.
  • First-timers: pick one mod chain to learn (Mekanism → AE2 is the classic), ignore the rest until it matters.

Verdict

ATM10 earns its S-tier on scale, stability and the fact that it is the only modern kitchen sink with a satisfying finish line. It loses points on progression design because there essentially isn't any, and on performance because 500 mods is 500 mods. If you are choosing a pack for a server full of people who want different things, stop reading and install it. If you want a pack that plays you back, look at StoneBlock 4, an expert pack, or a themed pack from the rest of this guide.

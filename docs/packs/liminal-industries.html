What's great

  • A genuinely new premise: no dirt, no trees — recycle chairs for wood, cut carpet for string, strip wallpaper for glue, burn furniture for ash
  • Liminal atmosphere done right: no random mob spawns, harmless mimics, endless procedurally-tiled rooms, a library dimension — eerie, never cheap horror
  • Excellent quest book and custom tech tree; item pipes disabled until ME so you *must* learn conveyors and logistics
  • Custom mechanics fit the theme: Reality Anchors for chunkloading, wall piercers, cardboard armour that lets you crawl through gaps
  • Stable, responsive developer, active community, 1,400-upvote Reddit love

What holds it back

  • Create is 90% of the tech; if you dislike Create, skip it
  • Early game is tedious — hand-cranked power, scarce food, hunting furniture for wood — for the first 10–15 h
  • Automation needs space and most rooms are small; picking a huge room for your base is essential and not obvious
  • No map: chalk markings (walls regenerate) are the only navigation; thematically perfect, practically annoying
  • Botania and Eidolon arrive abruptly; some texture cohesion issues

Play this if…

  • Create fans who want a fresh setting and hard, thematic resource puzzles
  • Players who love the Backrooms / liminal-space aesthetic
  • Anyone bored of skyblock-in-a-different-biome packs — this one is thematic all the way through

Skip it if…

  • You don't enjoy Create
  • You want combat, exploration or open-world building
  • You need a minimap to stay sane

The short version

Liminal Industries was the surprise hit of 2025 and the most-upvoted modpack recommendation in the 2026 "top packs" thread after GTNH. You wake in the Backrooms — infinite yellow office rooms, buzzing lights, damp carpet — with nothing. There are no trees. There is no dirt. Wood comes from breaking chairs and tables; string from cutting carpet with a knife; glue from stripping wallpaper with heat; ash (for the pack's "ash alloy" starter tier) from literally setting furniture on fire and shovelling the residue. From there you build a Create-driven factory across five chapters, opening the way to a library dimension (the pack's Nether), an overworld you can eventually portal to, and industrial-scale automation inside rooms that were never meant for it.

It is one of the few packs on this site with a premise rather than a theme, and it commits to it in every recipe.

How it plays

Chapter 1 is scavenging. You learn the Backrooms' rules — carpet and wallpaper regenerate slowly, walls can't be broken (yet), nothing spawns randomly, and the few hostile things (sculk-spawner rooms, silverfish) are avoidable — while gathering furniture and hand-cranking your first Create machines. Food is scarce and the pack wants you slightly desperate. Players consistently describe this stretch as tedious and as the most memorable.

Chapters 2–3 open the Infinite Library (a bookshelf dimension with redstone, lodestones and loot that melts to iron), bamboo (your first renewable wood — automate it immediately), the alchemy catalyst, Immersive Engineering for coke and creosote (a treated-wood → livingwood → coke loop stretches scarce planks), light Botania (pure daisies, a few runes), and the first real production lines. Item pipes are disabled until you reach ME controllers late in chapter 4, so belts, chutes, funnels, IE conveyors and item routers are your logistics — a deliberate constraint that makes the pack feel like A&B rather than a kitchen sink.

Chapters 4–5 bring AE2, Reality Frames and Reality Anchors (chunk loading with a theme), Eidolon (its Soul Enchanter bypasses vanilla enchanting — a huge quality-of-life unlock), shadow steel, wraith summoning, and the industrial endgame.

Navigation is diegetic: chalk (clay + ash) on walls, cobble arrows, compass + lodestone. Carpet cuts regenerate in newer versions, so early "trail" habits stop working — a change that annoyed some long-time players. Cardboard armour lets you crawl 1-block gaps; a water bucket also works; a Wall Piercer eventually lets you cut through.

Design quality

The Reddit love is earned. The quest book is clear, the custom recipes are coherent, the dev (Appocryptha) is responsive and iterating on things like room RNG, and the atmosphere is sustained — a Chairold mimic that teleports away when you approach is unsettling and harmless, which is exactly the right note. It is also stable for a young pack.

The Create problem

The most common negative review says it plainly: "If you don't like Create, pass on this pack. 95% of the areas you can build in are tiny rooms and hallways, and this pack requires you to build large Create setups." Both halves are true. The counter-advice from finishers: nothing spawns, so claim the largest room you can find (tens of blocks high — they exist and are common) or the crude-oil room, and move early. Even so, Create's spatial appetite fights the setting, and that tension is the pack's defining trade-off.

Botania's necessity is debated (one camp: "VERY important", another: "a few runes and pure daisies"); the honest answer is it's light but mandatory.

Performance & multiplayer

6 GB is comfortable; Forge 1.20.1 with 146 mods is mid-weight. Room generation is cheap compared to overworld terrain. Multiplayer works but the pack is built around one factory; groups should share a base.

How it compares

  • vs Create: Above and Beyond: the same "design lines, mass-produce components" DNA in a radically different setting, with better Create QoL (1.20.1) and a stronger atmosphere. A&B is stricter; LI is stranger.
  • vs FTB StoneBlock 4: SB4 is broad, multi-mod and forgiving; LI is narrow, Create-centric and demanding.
  • vs Create: Astral: Astral teaches Create; LI assumes you can already think in belts.

Verdict

Liminal Industries is the most creative tech pack in years and, for Create players, an S-tier must-play. The tedious opening and the cramped-rooms-versus-big-machines tension are real, and the Create dependency is total. Accept those terms and you'll get something no other pack offers: a factory that hums in the hollow fluorescence of a place that shouldn't exist.

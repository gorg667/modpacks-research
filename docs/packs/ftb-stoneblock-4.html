What's great

  • The World Engine — a 15-stage multiblock ‘town hall’ you level to unlock recipes — gives the whole pack a spine and a sense of growth
  • Handcrafted Vaults (puzzle, combat, endurance; including a Portal-gun homage) are the best custom dungeons FTB has shipped
  • Unearthers replace sieving with villager-powered refining that you feed, upgrade and automate — refreshing after a decade of Ex Nihilo
  • Genuinely a modpack, not ‘Create plus friends’: Mekanism, AE2, Oritech, Ars, Occultism, Draconic, Twilight Forest all matter
  • Smooth, well-paced chapters 1–5 with branching paths; five starting islands; Echo NPC guides and shops
  • Excellent multiplayer via FTB Teams; works well on modest hardware for a 1.21 pack

What holds it back

  • Final chapter hits a wall the earlier quests don't prepare you for (time-liquid and dimensional-shard sinks) — patched somewhat since 1.1
  • Random loot rewards can hand you an orange Apotheosis weapon or portal gun that trivialises combat early
  • Magic mods (Occultism, Malum, Ars) are present but barely required — you can skip them almost entirely
  • Some gating feels arbitrary (Sophisticated Storage upgrades locked behind shop currency while stronger storage is free)
  • Purists miss the claustrophobic ‘alone in the rock’ feel — the Garden of Stone hub and early teleports open things up fast
  • Requires enable-command-block=true on servers; some vault bugs in specific versions (1.6)

Play this if…

  • Players who liked StoneBlock 1–2 or Sky Factory and want a modern, more curated version of that loop
  • Groups of 2–5 who want a shared goal that finishes in a couple of months
  • Newer modded players who want structure without expert-pack punishment
  • Anyone tired of ‘Create as the only tech mod’ packs

Skip it if…

  • You want expert difficulty — this is deliberately accessible
  • You dislike RNG loot bags and chicken/cow resource generation
  • You want to build sprawling open-world bases (you live in a cave hub until late)

The short version

StoneBlock 4 is the sequel FTB needed. After StoneBlock 3 was widely written off as forgettable ("practically zero thought put in it" in one popular difficulty-ranking thread) and OceanBlock 2 was criticised as an under-gated crafting simulator, SB4 arrived in late October 2025 with actual custom design: a KubeJS-scripted World Engine that anchors progression, handcrafted trial dungeons, a villager-driven resource-refining system, and a narrative layer delivered by holographic "Echoes". Within two weeks it had 150-upvote "I finished it" posts, it dominated the March 2026 "top modpacks of 2026" thread, and by May 2026 it was ranked #2 in the world behind only ATM10 — pulling players off ATM in its first weeks.

It is not an expert pack and does not pretend to be. It is a 100-hour guided progression pack that respects your time, and in 2026 that is rarer than it should be.

What you actually do

You wake in stone. Below you lies the Garden of Stone, an amethyst-lit hub where you choose one of five starting islands (the classic stone box, or livelier biome variants). From there, six Foundation Chapters with branching questlines unlock chambers, awaken Echoes, and level the World Engine.

The World Engine is the star. It's a massive relic introduced in chapter one that you upgrade through 15+ multiblock stages, each unlocking new crafting tiers and "lost technologies". Leveling it feels like upgrading a town hall in a strategy game — cinematic camera pans, new recipes, visible growth — and the endgame chapter has you feeding it enormous amounts of automated material (with a choice of tech or magic routes for some inputs, e.g. antimatter via Mekanism or Occultism).

Vaults are handcrafted trial dungeons hidden in the stone: logic puzzles, combat gauntlets, endurance runs. Several are set-pieces — a Portal-gun puzzle vault, a Pigstep dance party, an Ignis boss arena — and they reward progression materials you cannot get elsewhere. Almost every review, positive or negative, singles them out as the best part and asks for more.

Unearthers are FTB's answer to the sieve. Early you sift gravel/sand/dust with a brush; soon you build glass cubes housing villagers who refine debris for you as long as you feed and upgrade them. New professions (Archaeologist, Geologist, Dimensionalist) unlock different outputs. They work with veinmining, automate cleanly, and remain relevant late for rare drops.

Echoes are the shopkeepers and story: the Echo of Guidance, the Machinist, the Quartermaster and others sell items for pack currency, hand out quests, and deliver lore.

The mod list is a deliberately broad NeoForge 1.21.1 spread: Create, Mekanism, Applied Energistics 2 (with doubled channels), Ars Nouveau, Occultism, Industrial Foregoing, Immersive Engineering, Oritech, Powah, Draconic Evolution, Replication (heavily buffed), Actually Additions, Just Dire Things, Apotheosis (gated behind Gateways of Eternity), Twilight Forest, Chickens, Fluid Cows, Sophisticated Storage, Xycraft, plus FTB Quests and FTB Teams.

Where it stumbles

The most-cited criticism comes from players who finished: the last chapter changes the rules. Chapters 1–5 flow smoothly with no roadblocks; then the final World Engine tiers suddenly demand infrastructure nobody asked you to build — ten buckets of "time liquid" per item across 130+ items, dimensional shards by the thousand, Hydra blood. If you happened to automate those things out of curiosity you're fine; if not, you hit a wall. FTB added a Chapter 6 creative-item challenge in 1.1/1.2 and smoothed some sinks, but the pacing complaint remains valid.

Second: many mods are optional to the point of decorative. Occultism, Malum and Ars Nouveau have chapters but almost nothing requires them. FTB's design lead answered this directly — StoneBlock packs are "always a balancing act between letting people get into the mods they want and not forcing any" — and that's fair, but if you want E6E-style integration you won't find it here.

Third: RNG can break the curve. Reward bags can drop orange Apotheosis gear, portal guns and netherite basalt generators in the first hours; get lucky and every boss becomes trivial. Chickens, once bred, make raw resources a non-issue. Again, this is the StoneBlock tradition, and you can choose not to use the drops.

Finally, a vocal minority dislikes the direction: SB1–2 were about being trapped and clawing outward; SB4 gives you a lush hub, early teleporters and a pre-built mega-machine. If "helpless in the rock" was the appeal, this is a different feeling.

Performance & multiplayer

For a 1.21.1 pack it runs well — 6 GB is comfortable, 8 GB with shaders. Load times are a fraction of ATM10's. Multiplayer is first-class: FTB Teams shares quests and the World Engine per team, and the FTB App can spin up hosted servers directly. Server owners must set enable-command-block=true or vault structures break.

How it compares

  • vs FTB OceanBlock 2: OB2 is lighter (~36 h), less gated, more "craft the list". SB4 has better custom mechanics and a proper skeleton.
  • vs FTB Skies / Skies Expert: Skies is more traditional sieve skyblock; Skies Expert is much harder. SB4 sits between them in difficulty with far more bespoke content.
  • vs Sky Factory 4: SF4 is the beloved 1.12.2 classic with prestige and tree-based resources. SB4 is the modern equivalent with more narrative and less sandbox freedom.
  • vs ATM10: To the Sky: ATM's skyblock is a kitchen sink in the void; SB4 is a designed campaign.
  • vs StoneBlock 3: not close. SB4 is a full generation better.

Verdict

StoneBlock 4 is the strongest argument in years that FTB can still make designed packs. The World Engine, the Vaults and the Unearthers are genuinely new ideas executed with polish; chapters 1–5 are some of the best-paced guided content on modern Minecraft; and the whole thing finishes in a hundred hours, on a mid-range PC, with friends. The endgame wall and the take-it-or-leave-it magic mods keep it from perfection, but as a 2026 "what should we play next" answer for a group that doesn't want ATM again, it is the pick.
